--- slide: A Simple Thermodynamic System ---

	- Statistical mechanics makes predictions about properties and behaviour of systems whose number of particles is enormous
	
- The more particles, the more details are unknown
- For precise predictions, large number of particles improves the representativeness of averages, and their evolution

- Thermodynamic system is a system, whose number of particles is large enough for the predictions to become very precise

- Statistical mechanics:
	- 
		- classical
		- not 'modern' physics (i.\,e. not based on quantum mechanics)
		- BUT supports many areas of physics!

--- slide: Entropy and Temperature ---

	- Entropy $S$ is a quantity proportional to the logarithm of the 'number of ways' $W$ (i.\,e. to the log of multiplicity $\Omega$). Boltzmann constant $k_B$ sets the dimensions for the entropy $S$
	\[
S  = k_{B} \ln W \qquad \& \qquad S  =  k_{B} \ln \Omega \qquad \& \qquad [S]=\frac{J}{K}
\]

- We saw that the system has a tendency to evolve towards equilibrium, i.\,e. towards highest $W$

- According to the second law of thermodynamics,  entropy tends to increase (in a closed system): 
\[
\Delta S \geq 0
\]
Considering the number of ways $W$: 
\[
\Delta S  = S_{f}  - S_{i}  = k_{B} [  \ln W_{f} -\ln W_{i} ]  = k_{B} \ln \frac{W_{f}}{W_{i}} 
\]

--- slide: Entropy and Temperature ---

\footnote{{Recap the definitions for isolated, closed, and open systems.}} 
{8.5cm}

	- Consider two bodies, 1 and 2, in thermal contact. Heat will flow, and entropy will change:
\[ 
 d S_{t o t} = d S_{1}  + d S_{2} 
 \]
Assume that entropy changes due to changes in the energy (ok, since here, only heat

- flows), so $S=S(E)$, and therefore
\[ 
 d S =  \bigg( \frac{\partial S}{\partial E} \bigg) \,d E 
 \]
- Thus $d S_{t o t} = ( \frac{\partial S}{\partial E} )_{2} d E_{2}  +  (  \frac{\partial S}{\partial E} )_{1} d E_{1}$,
and since energy is transferred only between 1 and 2, condition $d E_{1}=-d E_{2}$ applies leading to
\[ 
d S_{tot} =   [   \bigg( \frac{\partial S}{\partial E}  \bigg)_{2} -  \bigg( \frac{\partial S}{\partial E}  \bigg)_{1}  \;] d E_{2}
 \]

[FIGURE: SOM_Figure_4.png]

--- slide: Entropy and Temperature ---

	- At equilibrium the two bodies will have the same temperature

- We also know that when equilibrium is reached,  the entropy $S$ does not increase anymore, so $d S_{t o t}=0$ resulting in 
\[ 
  \bigg(  \frac{\partial S}{\partial E}  \bigg)_{2} =   \bigg( \frac{\partial S}{\partial E}  \bigg)_{1} 
 \]
- The above condition leads to a convenient definition for the temperature: 

- 
	- At equilibrium the temperatures are equal $T_1  =   T_2 \equiv T$
	- We can define temperature $T$ via equation 
	 \[
	 \frac{1}{T} \equiv \bigg( \frac{\partial S}{\partial E} \bigg)\,, \qquad \,~where \qquad S  = k_{B} \ln W
	 \]

--- slide: Entropy and Temperature ---

- How can we take a derivative of $S$ with respect to $E$? 

	- In a physical system usually also the number of possibilities (number of 'ways' $W$) is a function of energy (more energy, higher multiplicity) so there is no contradiction

- In many cases $W=W(E)$ and thus also the entropy $S=S(E)$
- Recalling that the change in energy $dE$ is equal to the heat transferred between objects ($dE=dQ$),  we get 
\[
	 \frac{1}{T}  =   \frac{\partial S}{\partial E}  \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad dS  =  \frac{dQ}{T}  
	 \]
allowing us to calculate the change of entropy $\Delta S$ of the system:
\[ 
 \Delta S  = S_{2}  -  S_{1} = \int_{i}^{f} \frac{d Q}{T} 
 \]

--- slide: Einstein's solid ---

	- Einstein proposed a simple model for solids: 
	- 
	- Atoms in a crystal can oscillate along three directions
	- Atoms oscillate independently of nearby atoms (assumption/approximation) 
	- Equilibrium state is determined by the crystalline structure 
	- Oscillators are harmonic (assumption/approximation), i.\,e. comply with Hooke's law:  $F=-kx$

--- slide: Einstein's solid ---

	- A solid body where $N$ atoms form a set of $3N$ oscillators
- All have the same ground state frequency which depends on the crystal structure
- Treating oscillators quantum mechanically, the allowed energy states for harmonic oscillators occur with spacing of $h f_{0}$, starting from $1 / 2 h f_{0}$ 

{7.5cm}

	- Energies of a single oscillator are
\[ 
 \varepsilon_{j} = ( j  +\frac{1}{2} )\,  h f_{0} 
 \]
- $j=0 \Leftrightarrow$ ground state
\[ 
 \varepsilon_{j=0} = \frac{1}{2} h f_{0} 
 \]
i.\,e. $j=$~Energy state index

[FIGURE: Tikz_SOM_Statistical_figures.pdf]

[FIGURE: Tikz_SOM_Statistical_figures.pdf]

--- slide: Einstein's solid ---

	- Number of allowed states have in principle no upper limit

[FIGURE: Tikz_SOM_Statistical_figures.pdf]
 
	- Two or more oscillators can be in the same state
	- The harmonic oscillators in the solid body continuously exchange quanta of $h f_{0}$ with each other (e.\,g. via thermal excitations)

[FIGURE: SOM_Figure_5.png]

--- slide: Einstein's solid ---

	- Return to the system of harmonic \\
		oscillators exchanging energy\\
		quanta $\Leftrightarrow$ Einstein's solid

	- Assume the system contains $N$\\
	 identical but separately identifiable\\ 
	 (=\,distinguishable) harmonic oscillators

- Energy of $i$:th oscillator (for simplicity,\\
	counted from ground level...) is given by
\[ 
 E_{n_{i}}  =  n_{i} \hbar \omega_{0} \quad ( n_{i} = 0,1,2, \ldots )  
\]
And hereby, total energy of the system ($N$ oscillators):
\[
 E  = \sum_{i=1}^{N}  E_{n_{i}}  = \sum_{i=1}^{N} n_{i} \hbar \omega_{0}  = M \hbar \omega_{0} \quad \text { (here: } M=\sum_{i=1}^{N} n_{i} ) 
\]

[FIGURE: SOM_Figure_5.png]

--- slide: Einstein's solid ---

	- At thermal equilibrium, the oscillators in the system have different energies, in fact, form an energy distribution

- The probability for a single oscillator to be in a given energy state $i$ is obtained from statistical analysis 

- The probability to obtain subset $a$:
\[ 
 P_{a} = \frac{\text { number of ways to obtaining subset a }}{\text { total number of ways of obtaining all possibilities }} 
 \]
- Here we assume that the probability for a certain subset of the system follows the number of ways $W$ to have arrangements within that subset 

- We also assume that each 'way', i.\,e. each microstate, is equally probable

--- slide: Einstein's solid ---

- So for the oscillators, which in our example form an Einstein's solid, the probability that an oscillator $i$ contains energy $E_{n_{i}}$ $(=n_{i} $ quanta) will be
\[ 
 P_{n_{i}} = \frac{\text { number of ways energy can be distributed with fixed } n_{i}}{\text { total number of ways energy can be distributed }} 
 \]

- The above equation gives us conceptual understanding of the situation, but not yet simple tools to calculate things
\implies{ more detailed statistical analysis is necessary }

- The total number of ways how $N$ quantum numbers can form the integer $M = \sum_{i=1}^N n_i$ is given by binomial coefficient
\[
\Omega(N, M)  = \frac{(M+N-1) !}{M !(N-1) !} \equiv (\!\! {c}
M+N-1 \\
M
  \!\! ) 
\]

--- slide: Einstein's solid ---

- The number of ways the energy can be distributed for a fixed $n_i$ is given by 
\[
 (\!\! {c}
 ( M-n_{i} ) +(N-1)-1 \\
 ( M-n_{i} ) 
 \!\!) 
\]

{6cm}

-  in total resulting in
	\[
P_{n_{i}}  =  \frac{ (\!\! {c}
 ( M-n_{i} ) +(N-1)-1 \\
 ( M-n_{i} ) 
  \!\!)  }{
 (\!\! {c}
M+N-1 \\
M
 \!\!) }
\]

--- slide: Boltzmann & Maxwell-Boltzmann distributions ---

	- Applying equation for $P_{n_{i}}$, shown 1_3_Pn{on earlier slide}, is tedious. For large systems, it is feasible to use Boltzmann probability:
\[ 
 P ( E_{n} )  = A e^{-E_{n} / k_{B} T} 
 \]
It is seen in Figure 6 that Boltzmann works fairly well even for smaller systems like the one given

{5.5cm}

	- For $P ( E_{n} ) $ to be a qualified probability function, it needs to be normalized, i.\,e. we need to choose constant $A$ properly

[FIGURE: SOM_Figure_6.png]

--- slide: Boltzmann & Maxwell-Boltzmann distributions ---

- \[

\sum P ( E_{n} ) &  =  \sum_{n} A e^{-E_{n} / k_{B} T}  =  1 \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad A  = \frac{1}{\sum_{n} e^{-E_{n} / k_{B} T}} \\
 \Rightarrow \qquad \quad P ( E_{n} ) & = \frac{e^{-E_{n} / k_{B} T}}{\sum_{n} e^{-E_{n} / k_{B} T}}

\]
- And thus, e.g. average energy $< E >\,$$^*$ can be obtained by\footnote{{$^*$\;$< E >\,$ is the  expected value{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expected_value}, defined as $\mathrm{E}(X) \equiv \sum_{i=1}^n x_i p_i \,$. Memorize~this.}}
\[

 < E >\, &  = \sum_{n} E_{n} P ( E_{n} ) \\
 & = \sum_{n} E_{n} \frac{e^{-E_{n} / k_{B} T}}{\sum_{n} e^{-E_{n} / k_{B} T}} \\
 & = \frac{\sum_{n} E_{n} e^{-E_{n} / k_{B} T}}{\sum_{n} e^{-E_{n} / k_{B} T}}

\]

--- slide: Boltzmann & Maxwell-Boltzmann distributions ---

	- For the Einstein's solid we had $E_{n}=n \hbar \omega_{0}$, now let's plug this into above equation, and plot the Boltzmann distribution (Fig.~7)

[FIGURE: SOM_Figure_7.png]

- Variation of Boltzmann probability distribution $P ( E_{n} )$ for 3 different temperature ranges (from $k_{\mathrm{B}} T\!<\!\hbar \omega_{0}$ to $k_{\mathrm{B}} T\!>\!\hbar \omega_{0}$):

{6cm}

	- 
	- $T=\frac{1}{5} \hbar \omega_{0} / k_{B}$
- $T=\hbar \omega_{0} / k_{B}$
- $T=5 \hbar \omega_{0} / k_{B}$

- Obviously at lower temperatures it is very
	unlikely for the oscillators to possess
	several quanta (of $\hbar \omega_{0}$), i.\,e. to get
	to higher energy states

--- slide: Boltzmann & Maxwell-Boltzmann distributions ---

{7cm}

	- We want to often know how
		many particles (oscillators) are at
		certain state
		(possess a certain number of quanta)

- Total number of particles is still $N$

- We define occupation number $\mathbb{N}$: 
\[ 
 \mathbb{N} ( E_{n} ) _{B o l t z m a n n}  =  N P ( E_{n} )  =  N\, A e^{-E_{n} / k_{B} T} \,, \qquad  < E >\,  = \frac{\sum_{n} E_{n} \mathbb{N} ( E_{n} ) }{\sum_{n} \mathbb{N} ( E_{n} ) } 
 \]

- Using occupation number $\mathbb{N}$, we get:
\[ (\!\! 
{\footnotesize
{c}
\text {number of} \\ \text {particles of} \\ \text {energy E}
  }
 \!\!)   =  (\! 
{\footnotesize
{c}
\mathbb{N} \text {, number of} \\ particles in a given \\ 
\text {state of energy E}
}
\!\!)   \times  (\!\!  
{\footnotesize 
{c}
\text {number of} \\ states of \\ \text {energy E}
}
\!\!) 
\]

[FIGURE: SOM_Figure_7.png]

--- slide: Boltzmann & Maxwell-Boltzmann distributions ---

	- Now we can write: 
	\[
	< E >\,  = \frac{\sum_{n} E_{n} \mathbb{N} ( E_{n} ) }{\sum_{n} \mathbb{N} ( E_{n} ) }
	\]
- Often the quantum energy levels are so close to each other that the sums can be replaced by integrals

- Generally, the ratio between probabilities of two neighbouring energy levels is
\[ 
 \frac{P ( E_{n} ) }{P ( E_{n+1} ) }  = \frac{e^{-E_{n} / k_{B} T}}{e^{-E_{n+1} / k_{B} T}} = e^{ ( E_{n+1}-E_{n} )  / k_{B} T} 
 \]
- So if $E_{n+1}-E_{n}$ is much smaller than $k_{B} T$, then the ratio really is close to 1, and the sum can be transformed into an integral

--- slide: Boltzmann & Maxwell-Boltzmann distributions ---

	- This could be seemingly performed by taking the limit
\[ 
  < E >\,  = \frac{\sum_{n} E_{n} \mathbb{N} ( E_{n} ) }{\sum_{n} \mathbb{N} ( E_{n} ) } \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad \frac{\int_{n} E \mathbb{N}(E) d n}{\int_{n} \mathbb{N}(E) d n} 
 \]
	- However, we need to be careful when transforming sums into continuous integrals
	
- First of all, the quantum number $n$ is not necessarily the most convenient integration variable here, energy is a better quantity

- Therefore we should have:
\[ 
  < E >\,  = \frac{\int E \mathbb{N}(E) d n}{\int \mathbb{N}(E) d n} = \frac{\int E \mathbb{N}(E) \frac{d n}{d E} d E}{\int \mathbb{N}(E) \frac{d n}{d E} d E} = \frac{\int E \mathbb{N}(E) D(E) d E}{\int \mathbb{N}(E) D(E) d E} 
 \]

--- slide: Boltzmann & Maxwell-Boltzmann distributions ---

- 
\[ 
  < E >\,  = \frac{\int E \mathbb{N}(E) d n}{\int \mathbb{N}(E) d n}  = \frac{\int E \mathbb{N}(E) \frac{d n}{d E} d E}{\int \mathbb{N}(E) \frac{d n}{d E} d E} = \frac{\int E \mathbb{N}(E) D(E) d E}{\int \mathbb{N}(E) D(E) d E} 
 \]
- Here we have introduced a quantity known as the density of states $D(E)$, indicating how closely the energy levels are packed:
\[ 
 D(E) \equiv \frac{\small differential number of states within range dE close to E}{d E} = \frac{d n}{d E} 
 \]

- In practise, most (if not all) calculations of mean values are calculated as integrals
\implies[\normal]{ Density of states $D$ (or DOS) is a central and omnipresent \\ concept in solid-state physics! }
\implies[\normal]{ Re-introduced in our course on solid-state physics.. }

--- slide: Boltzmann & Maxwell-Boltzmann distributions ---

- Harmonic oscillator provides an easy example to demonstrate how the density of states $D(E)$ can be calculated: 
\[

E_{n}  = n \hbar \omega_{0} \qquad &\Rightarrow& \qquad
\frac{d n}{d E} &= \frac{differential number of states}{d E} \\
&\Rightarrow& \qquad d n &  = \frac{1}{\hbar \omega_{0}} d E \\
&\Rightarrow &    \frac{d n}{d E} & = \frac{1}{\hbar \omega_{0}}  = D(E)
 
\]

- Admittedly, this example is somewhat boring/trivial, as it gives a constant density of states, but at least we can verify that the result makes sense (energy states of harmonic oscillator are equispaced)

--- slide: Boltzmann & Maxwell-Boltzmann distributions ---

	- For a set of identical (but distinguishable) harmonic oscillators, we obtain the average energy:
\[ 
  < E >\,  = \frac{\int E \mathbb{N}(E) D(E) d E}{\int \mathbb{N}(E) D(E) d E}  = \frac{\int E N A e^{-E_{n} / k_{B} T} \frac{1}{\hbar \omega_{0}} d E}{\int N A e^{-E_{n} / k_{B} T} \frac{1}{\hbar \omega_{0}} d E}  = \ldots = k_{B} T 
 \]
- Previously we obtained a result:
\[ 
  < E >\,  = \frac{\hbar \omega_{0}}{e^{\hbar \omega_{0} / k_{B} T}-1} 
 \]
- If we have $\hbar \omega_{0} \ll k_{B} T$, then $x=\frac{\hbar \omega_{0}}{k_{B} T} \ll 1$, and using $e^{x} \approx 1+x$, we obtain $ < E > \approx k_{B} T$

--- slide: Classical Averages ---

	- Let's examine the system of an ideal gas, where the energy of a single gas molecule is
\[
E  = \frac{1}{2} m ( v_{x}^{2}+v_{y}^{2}+v_{z}^{2} )  = \frac{1}{2} m v^{2}
\]

- We assume that there is a probability distribution of speeds of the gas molecules, but at this point we do not yet know the form of this distribution

- To get an average of an arbitrary (yet unspecified) function of speed $f=f(v)$, we need to take integrals over space and velocity, i.\,e. 3 components of the velocity vector

--- slide: Classical Averages ---

- The spatial integrations over volume $V'$ cancel out, so we obtain:
\[
\overline{f(v)}  = \frac{ \int f(v) e^{-\frac{1}{2} m v^{2} / k_{B} T} d V' d v_{x} d v_{y} d v_{z}}{ \int e^{-\frac{1}{2} m v^{2} / k_{B} T} d V' d v_{x} d v_{y} d v_{z} }  = \frac{ \int f(v) e^{-\frac{1}{2} m v^{2} / k_{B} T} d v_{x} d v_{y} d v_{z}}{ \int e^{-\frac{1}{2} m v^{2} / k_{B} T} d v_{x} d v_{y} d v_{z}}
\]
Because we are searching for the speed distribution (not velocity), it is convenient to transfer from cartesian to spherical coordinates using: 
\[
d v_{x} d v_{y} d v_{z}  = v^{2} d v \sin \theta \,d \theta \,d \phi
\]

--- slide: Classical Averages ---

	- Therefore, the integral turns into:
	\[
	\overline{f(v)}  =  \frac{ \int f(v) e^{-\frac{1}{2} m v^{2} / k_{B} T} v^{2} d v \sin \theta d \theta d  \phi }{ \int e^{-\frac{1}{2} m v^{2} / k_{B} T} v^{2} d v \sin \theta d \theta d  \phi }
	 =  \frac{ \int_{0}^{\infty} f(v) e^{-\frac{1}{2} m v^{2} / k_{B} T} v^{2} d v}{ \int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-\frac{1}{2} m v^{2} / k_{B} T} v^{2} d v}
	\]
	
 where the integrals of the denominator, and over variables $\theta$ and $\phi$ can be solved to obtain
\[
\overline{f(v)}  =  \int_{0}^{\infty}  f(v) [ \sqrt{2/\pi} \bigg( \frac{m}{k_{B} T}  \bigg)^{3 / 2} v^{2} e^{-\frac{1}{2} m v^{2} / k_{B} T}] d v
\]

- The integrand in the formula can be seen as something resembling a probability distribution $P=P(v)$:
\[
\overline{f(v)}  =  \int f(v) d P =  \int f(v) \frac{d P}{d v} d v
\]

--- slide: Classical Averages ---

- Thus the quantity inside brackets may be interpreted as the probability distribution for the speed of gas molecules
\[
P(v)_{\text {Maxwell}}  =  \frac{d P}{d v}  =  \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}   \bigg( \frac{m}{k_{B} T} \bigg)^{3 / 2} v^{2} e^{-\frac{1}{2} m v^{2} / k_{B} T}
\]
- If we add the number density of the gas molecules $n=N / V$ into this formula, we obtain a distribution which has units of particles per unit volume, but which contains information of the probability distribution of the speeds for gas molecules
\[

	- From quantum mechanics we recall that wave functions must obey the below rules:

- Bosons: {\small The wave functions of identical bosons must be symmetric if two bosons are interchanged. Assume we have two identical bosons in the single particle states $n$ and $n'$ and let the $\psi_{n}$ and $\psi_{n'}$ be the wave functions of the two states. If we label two bosons 1 and 2, then the state of the combined two-particle system is described by a wave function of form $\psi_{n}(1) \psi_{n'}(2)$.
 But, since the particles are indistinguishable, we could equally write the wave function of this state as $\psi_{n}(2) \psi_{n'}(1)$. We cannot insist on calling the bosons in state $n$ and state $n'$ by the labels 1 and 2, respectively. The total wave function of this state must be symmetric:}
\[
\psi_{B E}(1,2)  = \psi_{S}  = \psi_{n}(1) \psi_{n'}(2)  + \psi_{n'}(1) \psi_{n}(2) \, \qquad (symmetric)
\]

- Fermions: {\small For identical fermions, on the other hand, the wave function must be antisymmetric:} 
\[
\quad \psi_{F D}(1,2)  = \psi_{A}  = \psi_{n}(1) \psi_{n^{\prime}}(2)  - \psi_{n'}(1) \psi_{n}(2) \, \qquad (antisymmetric)
\]

	- Let us compute the density of states $D(E)$ for the 3D oscillator 
	
	- The energy levels are known to be:
\[
E_{n_{x}, n_{y}, n_{z}}  =  (n_{x}^{2} +n_{y}^{2} +n_{z}^{2} ) \frac{\pi^{2} \hbar^{2}}{2 m L^{2}}
\]
and using:
\[
 n  \equiv \sqrt{n_{x}^{2}+n_{y}^{2}+n_{z}^{2}}
\]
- we get the energy of the oscillator as a function of quantum number $n$ [[$E(n)$]], and vice versa the quantum number as a function of energy $n=n(E)$:
\[
E  = \frac{\pi^{2} \hbar^{2}}{2 m L^{2}} n^{2} \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad n  = \sqrt{\frac{2 m L^{2} E}{\pi^{2} \hbar^{2}}}  =  n(E)
\]

--- slide: The Quantum Gas ---

{8cm}
	 \small
	- The illustrated (eighth of a sphere) is in $ (n_{x}, n_{y}, n_{z} )$ space, and the quantum numbers $n_{x}, n_{y}$ and $n_{z}$ can only have integer values 
	- If energy increases by $d E$ then the radius of the sphere will increase by $d n$, but the number of states will increase proportional to the volume of the added shell (=\;area of the shell $\times$ thickness $=4 \pi r^{2} d n$)

- We are dealing with only positive numbers, and due to symmetry of the problem we need to consider only $1 / 8$ of the spherical shell, which turn the differential number of states in range $d E$ to be equal to $\frac{1}{8} 4 \pi n^{2} d n$

- Thus, the density of states will be:
\[
 
D(E)  &=  \frac{\text { differential number of states in range } d E}{d E}  \\
	&= \frac{1}{8} 4 \pi n^{2} \frac{d n}{d E}

\]

[FIGURE: SOM_Figure_15.png]

--- slide: The Quantum Gas ---

	-  
	\[
\Rightarrow \qquad \qquad D(E)= \frac{1}{8} 4 \pi n^{2} \frac{d n}{d E} =\ldots= \frac{m^{3 / 2} L^{3}}{\pi^{2} \hbar^{3} \sqrt{2}} \sqrt{E} \qquad \qquad
\]
- Often it is more practical to deal with the volume of the well $(L^{3}=V )$ 

- Recall that two spin-1/2 particles can have the same energy. $D(E)$ should thus be multiplied with $(2 s+1)$:\\[-6mm]
\[
 \hspace{8cm} D(E) = \frac{(2 s+1) m^{3 / 2} V}{\pi^{2} \hbar^{3} \sqrt{2}} \sqrt{E}
\]

[FIGURE: Tikz_SOM_Statistical_figures.pdf]

	- We are interested of the average particle energy:\\
-  \small
	 - One could try to calculate the exact average particle energy 
	 - In general case, it proves to be tedious, since e.g. the parameters $B$ and $T$ depend on the system, systems are often complex and $D(E)$ prevents a closed form calculation of $B$ and $<E(T)>\,$ for a quantum gas

- But, we can at least derive an approximate solution for the average energy as a function of particle number density $N / V$ (Exercise):
\[
\overline{E}  = \frac{3}{2} k_{B} T [\! 1 \mp \frac{\pi^{3} \hbar^{3} \sqrt{2}}{ (2 s+1) (2 \pi m k_{B} T )^{3/2}  } ( \frac{N}{V} ) +\ldots ]\!\!
\]

- Thus, average energy depends both on temperature and particle number density
- With higher density $N/V$, energy increases for fermions $(+)$, decreases for bosons $(-)$

--- slide: The Quantum Gas ---

	- Classical particle behaviour $\leftrightarrow$ the 1st term dominates
$\leftrightarrow$ when particles have short (de Broglie) wavelength and large separation ($N/V$ is small)

- In terms of wavelength: 
\[
\lambda  = \frac{h}{p}  = \frac{h}{m v}  = \frac{h}{m \sqrt{3 k_{B} T / m}} \propto \frac{h}{\sqrt{m k_{B} T}} \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad \frac{1}{T^{3 / 2}} \propto \lambda^{3}
\]
- In terms of particle separation:
\[
\frac{d^{3}}{1} \propto \frac{V}{N} \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad d \propto (\frac{V}{N} )^{1 / 3}
\]

- We have a quantum term of form 
\[
\text {Quantum term}  \equiv \frac{\pi^{3} \hbar^{3} \sqrt{2}}{(2 s+1) (2 \pi m k_{B} T )^{\frac{3}{2}}} ( \frac{N}{V} )  \,\propto (\frac{\lambda}{d} )^{3}
\]

--- slide: The Quantum Gas ---

- We have a quantum term of form 
\[
\text {Quantum term}  \equiv \frac{\pi^{3} \hbar^{3} \sqrt{2}}{(2 s+1) (2 \pi m k_{B} T )^{\frac{3}{2}}}  ( \frac{N}{V} )  \propto (\frac{\lambda}{d} )^{3}
\]

- The 2nd term (the quantum term) in the approximate solution for the mean energy $\overline{E}$, is clearly:
- 
	 - small when $\lambda$ is small
- small when $\lambda<d$
- large when $\lambda$ is large
- large when $d$ is small

--- slide: The Quantum Gas ---

	- Another angle of approach discusses how the particles are spread to different energy levels 
	- We discuss fermions as an example, and calculate the Fermi energy $E_F$ at $T=0$

- Total number of particles: $\qquad N=\int_{0}^{\infty}  \mathbb{N} (E)_{F D} D(E) d E$

- At $T=0$ the occupation number $ \mathbb{N} $ is a step function for fermions ($ \mathbb{N} =1$ for $\mathrm{E}<E_{F}$,  $\mathbb{N} =0$ for $\mathrm{E}>E_{F}$) 
\[ 
 
&\Rightarrow& \qquad N  &=  \int_{0}^{E_{F}} \frac{1\cdot (2 s+1) m^{3 / 2} V}{\pi^{2} \hbar^{3} \sqrt{2}} \sqrt{E} d E  =  \frac{(2 s+1) m^{3 / 2} V}{\pi^{2} \hbar^{3} \sqrt{2}} ( \frac{2}{3} E_{F}^{3 / 2} ) \\ 
&\Rightarrow& \qquad E_{F}  &= \frac{\pi^{2} \hbar^{2}}{m} \Bigg[\frac{3}{(2 s+1) \pi 2^{1/2}} \frac{N}{V} \Bigg]^{2 / 3}
 
\]

--- slide: The Quantum Gas ---

	- The result indicates that even at zero temperature, energy increases with increasing fermion concentration: $\qquad E_{F} \propto (\frac{N}{V} )^{2 / 3}$

- Re-arrangement of terms results in
\[

& & E_{F}  &= \frac{\pi^{2} \hbar^{2}}{m} \Bigg[\frac{3}{(2 s+1) \pi 2^{1/2}} \frac{N}{V} \Bigg]^{2 / 3} \\
&\Rightarrow& \qquad E_{F}^{3 / 2}  &=   \bigg( \frac{\pi^{2} \hbar^{2}}{m}  \bigg)^{3 / 2} \frac{3}{(2 s+1) \pi 2^{1/2}} \frac{N}{V}  =  \frac{3 \pi^{2}}{2^{1/2}} \frac{\hbar^{3}}{(2 s+1) m^{3 / 2}} \frac{N}{V}

\]
- Thus, we see a proportionality $\frac{\hbar^{3}}{(2 s+1) m^{3 / 2}} \frac{N}{V} \propto E_{F}^{3 / 2}$ which can be used to analyze the quantum term in the equation for mean energy:
\[
\overline{E}  = \frac{3}{2} k_{B} T [\! 1 \mp \frac{\pi^{3} \hbar^{3} \sqrt{2}}{(2 s+1) (2 \pi m k_{B} T )^{\frac{3}{2}} (\frac{N}{V} ) }  +\ldots ]
\]

--- slide: The Quantum Gas ---

	- So, the quantum term is proportional to $\big( \frac{E_{F}}{k_{B} T}  \big)^{3 / 2}$
- In the case $k_{B} T \gg E_{F}$ the classical first term survives, and the second term, i.e the quantum term, is small 

- The particles are spread diffusely among different states, so quantum indistinguishability can be ignored
- Summarum: A gas will behave classically
If 
\[

&or if\quad& & \\
& & \qquad \frac{N}{V} \frac{\hbar^{3}}{ (m k_{B} T )^{3 / 2}} &\ll 1 \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad \text {Classical limit} \\

\]

[FIGURE: SOM_Tipler_figure.png]

--- slide: Photon Gas ---

	- How many 'lattice points' can we have in a $n$-space volume with radius $R$?
\[

& & R& = \frac{2 L E}{h c} \\ 
&\Rightarrow \qquad& N &= 2 \cdot \frac{1}{8} \cdot \frac{4}{3} \pi R^{3}  =  \frac{\pi}{3} ( \frac{2 L E}{h c} ) ^{3}  
=  \frac{8 \pi}{3} \frac{E^{3}}{h^{3} c^{3}} L^{3}  
= \frac{8 \pi}{3} \frac{E^{3}}{h^{3} c^{3}} V

\]

- where $N$ is the number of states within a sphere of radius $R=2 L E /(h c)$ 

- Now we can calculate the number of points within an infinitesimal energy range $d E$:

\[
\frac{d N}{d E}  = \frac{8 \pi}{3} \frac{3 E^{2}}{h^{3} c^{3}} V
 = \frac{8 \pi E^{2}}{h^{3} c^{3}} V 
 \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad 
 \frac{d N}{d f}  = \frac{8 \pi f^{2}}{c^{3}} V \qquad (E=h f \quad \Rightarrow \quad d E=h d f)
\]

--- slide: Photon Gas ---

	- So, to interpret the result, we have obtained the number of states within a certain frequency range:
\[
\frac{\text { different number of states in range df }}{d f}  = \frac{8 \pi V}{c^{3}} f^{2}
\]
- Or, if energy is considered:
\[
\frac{\text { different number of states in range dE }}{d E / h}  = \frac{8 \pi V}{c^{3}} (E / h)^{2}
\]

- Thus, for the density of states $D=D(E)$ in a system of photons (in a volume $V$), we obtain:
\[
D(E)  = \frac{8 \pi V}{h^{3} c^{3}} E^{2}
\]

--- slide: Photon Gas ---

	- Note, that the photon gas differs from the harmonic oscillator, or e.\,g. liquid helium case

- In the photon gas, we do not actually fix the number of photons beforehand, since all systems absorb and emit photons continuously, and therefore the temporary number of photons is rather dictated by the physics of the system and its conditions

- The Bose–Einstein statistics for bosons were given by: 
\[ \mathbb{N} (E)_{B E}  =  \frac{1}{B e^{E / k_{B} T}-1}
\] 
- where the parameter $B$ should be linked to number of particles $N$ 

- Since $N$ is not fixed, a solution is to set $B\equiv1$ (for photon gas)
\[
 \mathbb{N} (E)_{B E, \text { photon } g a s}=\frac{1}{e^{E / k_{B} T}-1}
\]

--- slide: Photon Gas ---

	- Q: Compare quantitatively the internal energies in electromagnetic radiation and matter
- A: We could calculate the average energy as given before: 
\[
<E>\,  =  \frac{\int E  \mathbb{N} (E) D(E) d E}{\int  \mathbb{N} (E) D(E) d E}
\]
- where the nominator stands for the energy of the system (and denumerator stands for total number of particles) 

- But, we could as well use only the nominator to obtain the internal energy of EM-radiation, or 'photon gas' 

--- slide: Photon Gas ---

- Using the change of variables, $E  = k_{B} T x \quad \Rightarrow \quad x  = E/(k_{B} T)$, the nominator turns into:
\[

U_{photon}  &=  \int_{0}^{\infty} E  \mathbb{N} (E) D(E) d E
 =  
 \int_{0}^{\infty} E \frac{1}{e^{E / k_{B} T}-1} \frac{8 \pi V}{h^{3} c^{3}} E^{2} d E \\
& = 
\frac{8 \pi V}{h^{3} c^{3}} \int_{0}^{\infty} k_{B} T x \frac{1}{e^{x}-1} ( k_{B} T x ) ^{2} k_{B} T d x
 = 
 \frac{8 \pi V k_{B}^{4} T^{4}}{h^{3} c^{3}} \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{x^{3} d x}{e^{x}-1}  \\
 &= 
 \frac{8 \pi V k_{B}^{4} T^{4}}{h^{3} c^{3}} \cdot \frac{\pi^{4}}{15}

\]

--- slide: Photon Gas ---

	- So, for photon gas we obtain 
	\[
	U_{p h o t o n}=\frac{8 \pi V k_{B}^{4} T^{4}}{h^{3} c^{3}} \cdot \frac{\pi^{4}}{15}  = \frac{8 \pi^{5} V k_{B}^{4} T^{4}}{15 h^{3} c^{3}}
	\]
- At temperature of $300 \mathrm{~K}$ we obtain a value of: $\quad \frac{U_{p h o t o n}}{V} =6.1 \times 10^{-6} \mathrm{~J} / \mathrm{m}^{3}$

- For ordinary (monatomic ideal) gas the internal energy at NTP is given by
\[
\frac{U_{\text {material }}}{V}
 = 
 \frac{N \times \frac{3}{2} k_{B} T}{V}
  = 
  \frac{\frac{3}{2} p V}{V} = \frac{3}{2} p = 1.5 \times 10^{5} \mathrm{~J} / \mathrm{m}^{3}
\]
- So, the ratio is small: $\frac{U_{\text {photon }}}{U_{\text {matter }}}=4 \times 10^{-11}$

 \implies{ Thus, we conclude that under NTP conditions the fraction of \\
 		thermodynamic system's total energy in the form of \\
		electromagnetic radiation is very small }

--- slide: Photon Gas ---

	- We may interpret the integrand in the previous example for internal energy as the (total) energy of the system within a certain photon energy range [[$E , E+d E$]]:
\[
d U_{photon}(E, E+d E)
 = 
 E \frac{1}{e^{E / k_{B} T}-1} \frac{8 \pi V}{h^{3} c^{3}} E^{2} d E
  = \frac{h f^{3}}{e^{h f / k_{B} T}-1} \frac{8 \pi V}{c^{3}} d f
\]
- this is the Planck's law, giving us the

{6.5cm}

	- spectral energy density for electromagnetic radiation or so called blackbody radiation

- The maximum peak of the curve moves linearly with temperature, following the Wien's law 

[FIGURE: SOM_Figure_20.png]

--- slide: Photon Gas ---

	- Integration of the area under the curve gives the Stefan–Boltzmann's law:
\[
U \propto T^{4}
\]
- To summarize, the bosonic photon gas model allows to derive many of the foundational equations/laws connecting thermodynamics with electrodynamics
- 
	 - Planck's law
	 - Blackbody radiation
	 - Wien's law
	 - Stefan–Boltzmann's law

[FIGURE: SOM_Figure_20.png]

## 1.9 The Laser

--- slide: 1. Statistical Mechanics ---

    -<1-> 1_1_a{A Simple Thermodynamic System}
    -<1-> 1_2_a{Entropy and Temperature}	
    -<1-> 1_3_a{Einstein's solid}	
    -<1-> 1_4_a{Boltzmann & Maxwell-Boltzmann distributions}
    -<1-> 1_5_a{Classical Averages}
    -<1-> 1_6_a{Quantum Distributions}
    -<1-> 1_7_a{The Quantum Gas}

    -<1-> 1_8_a{Photon Gas}
	-<1-> 1_9_a{The Laser}
	-<1-> 1_10_a{Specific Heats}
	-<1-> 1_11_a{Debye Model}

--- slide: The Laser ---

{8.1cm}

	- Lasers are a good example of a photon gas system
	- They are devices where high energy density of electromagnetic radiation is introduced within a cavity, outputting a coherent unidirectional monochromatic beam of light

- The basic components are:

\small
	- A three-level system in which atoms in the upper level "2" make a transition to the lower level "1" that is stimulated by the presence of photons of the right frequency
- An optical cavity to provide an environment with the right kind of photons
- Pumping mechanism to re-populate the upper level "2" for repeated operation

[FIGURE: SOM_Figure_21.png]

--- slide: The Laser ---

	- Practical operating principles of laser:

-  \small
	- Parallel mirrors are placed at the medium's two ends
  - Photons not parallel the axis have relatively few opportunities to induce stimulated emission
  - Photons parallel to axis reflect back and forth, providing many opportunities
  - The length $L$ is tuned to standing wave: $L=n \lambda / 2$

{6.25cm}

-   \small
  - A resonance is formed inside this optical cavity
  - Parallel radiation gets amplified
  - Off-axis radiation diminishes
  - Laser wavelength becomes narrower than natural line width
  - More energy can be fed into the laser by an 'optical pumping process'

[FIGURE: SOM_Figure_23.png]

--- slide: The Laser ---

{7cm}

\small
	- Spontaneous emission occurs when an electron in a higher atomic energy level drops to lower level in a short but unpredictable time, emitting a photon

- Absorption occurs when an electron is promoted to higher atomic energy level by absorbing a photon

- In stimulated emission, an electron in higher energy level is influenced by a passing photon, whose energy is precisely the difference between the upper and lower level 
 - 
	- As a result, another photon with identical energy and wavelength to the first photon is emitted, and moreover in the same direction and with a correlated phase with the first one

[FIGURE: SOM_Figure_22.png]

--- slide: The Laser ---

{7cm}
\small 

	- How to describe these three processes mathematically?
For simplicity, gases are studied (simpler energy state treatment than in solids)

- In spontaneous emission, the rate $R_{spon}$ must be proportional to the number of atoms in the excited state, $N_{2}$, the proportionality constant being $A_{spon}$ ($R_{spon}=A_{\text {spon}} N_2$)

- Rate of absorption must be proportional to the number of particles in the lower level, $N_{1}$, but also proportional to the number of photons of the right energy which are entering the atom $Y(\Delta E)$, where $\Delta E=E_{2}-E_{1}$. The proportionality constant is $B_{abs}$ ($R_{abs}=B_{abs} N_{1} Y(\Delta E)$)

- Also for stimulated emission, we need the number of available atoms in the higher level, as well as the number of photons of right energy, which can trigger the emission ($R_{stim}=B_{stim} N_{2} Y(\Delta E)$)

[FIGURE: SOM_Figure_22.png]

--- slide: The Laser ---

	- The coefficients $A_{\text {spon}}$, $B_{abs}$ and $B_{\text {stim}}$ are known as the Einstein's coefficients 
	
	- In an equilibrium, these processes are in a balance:
\[

& & R_{\text {spon}} +R_{\text {stim}}  &= R_{abs} \\
 &\Rightarrow& \qquad A_{\text {spon}} N_{2} +B_{\text {stim}} N_{2} Y(\Delta E) & = B_{abs} N_{1} Y(\Delta E) \qquad \quad 

\]

- From where we solve for $Y(\Delta E)$, and using $N_{1} / N_{2}  = e^{-\frac{E_{1}}{k_{B} T}} / e^{-\frac{E_{2}}{k_{B} T}}$, we get:
\[

&\Rightarrow& 
\qquad Y(\Delta E) & = \frac{A_{\text {spon}} / B_{abs}}{\frac{N_{1}}{N_{2}}  - \frac{B_{\text {stim}}}{B_{abs}}}
 = 
 \frac{A_{\text {spon}} / B_{abs}}{\frac{e^{-E_{1} / k_{B} T}}{e^{-E_{2} / k_{B} T}}  - \frac{B_{stim}}{B_{abs}}} \\
 
&\Rightarrow&
 Y(\Delta E) & = \frac{A_{\text {spon}} / B_{abs}}{e^{\Delta E / k_{B} T}  - \frac{B_{\text {stim}}}{B_{abs}}}
  = 
  \frac{A_{\text {spon}} / B_{\text {stim}}}{\frac{B_{abs}}{B_{stim}} e^{\Delta E / k_{B} T}-1}  \qquad \quad 

\]

--- slide: The Laser ---

	- The quantity $Y(\Delta E)$ is not strictly speaking a number of photons entering the atom, but rather a photon gas spectral energy density for the quanta of right energy, given by Planck's law 
	
	- So, we can compare:
\[

Y(\Delta E) &= \frac{A_{\text {spon}} / B_{abs}}{\frac{N_{1}}{N_{2}} - \frac{B_{\text {stim}}}{B_{abs}}}
 = 
 \frac{A_{spon} / B_{abs}}{\frac{e^{-E_{1} / k_{B} T}}{e^{-E_{2} / k_{B} T}}-\frac{B_{\text {stim}}}{B_{abs}}}
  = 
  \frac{A_{\text {spon}} / B_{abs}}{e^{\Delta E / k_{B} T}  - \frac{B_{\text {stim}}}{B_{\text {abs }}}}
   = 
   \frac{A_{\text {spon}} / B_{\text {stim}}}{\frac{B_{abs}}{B_{\text {stim}}} e^{\Delta E / k_{B} T}-1} \\
d U_{photon}(\Delta E) &= 
 \frac{h f^{3}}{e^{\frac{\Delta E}{k_{B} T}}-1} \frac{8 \pi V}{c^{3}} d f \quad \Rightarrow \quad \frac{1}{V} \frac{d U_{photon}}{d f}
  = \frac{8 \pi}{c^{3}} \frac{h f^{3}}{e^{\frac{\Delta E}{k_{B} T}}-1}  =  Y(\Delta E)

\]
- Therefore, in equilibrium the below holds:
\[
B_{\text {stim}}=B_{abs} \qquad resulting~in \qquad \frac{A_{\text {spon}}}{B_{\text {stim}}}  = \frac{8 \pi h f^{3}}{c^{3}}
\]

--- slide: The Laser ---

	- Note, that condition $B_{stim}=B_{abs}$ means that an incoming photon is equally probable to trigger an absorption as a stimulated emission

- Frankly speaking, for lasers we do not favour this, we wish for the stimulation to dominate, since we wish to have a strong coherent beam, made by utilizing stimulated emission

[FIGURE: Tikz_SOM_Statistical_figures.pdf]

--- slide: The Laser ---

	- At normal temperature, in equilibrium, nearly all of the atoms are in the ground state 
	- Thus $N_{1} \gg N_{2}$, and for actual rates, it follows that in normal temperature $N_{1} B_{abs} \gg N_{2} B_{stim}$  
	\implies{ stimulated emission is not actually pronounced }

- Using optical pumping, i.\,e. an additional light source  with right frequency, the ground states can be excited into higher states 
\implies{ Population of a higher level with energy $E_{3}$ increases }

- If the medium of the laser (often a transparent solid) has a metastable state $E_{2}$, then many of the upper level $E_{3}$ states can drop down to this metastable state $E_{2}$ 
\implies{ Possible to create a situation where $N_{1}<N_{2}$ }

--- slide: The Laser ---

	- First, one uses optical pumping so that $N_{1} \approx N_{2} \approx N_{3}$ 
	- When the pumping is stopped, very soon afterwards, the level $E_{3}$ will depopulate, and soon there will be a high population of $E_{2}$, and also $N_{1}<N_{2}$
	
	- This phenomenon/condition is called population inversion 

{3.8cm}

	 - As a result, once the photon gas in the laser starts
	 to trigger more of the $E_{2}$
	 metastable level atoms,
	 eventually stimulated
	 emission rate will be
	 enhanced

[FIGURE: SOM_Figure_26.png]

## 1.10 Specific Heats

--- slide: 1. Statistical Mechanics ---

    -<1-> 1_1_a{A Simple Thermodynamic System}
    -<1-> 1_2_a{Entropy and Temperature}	
    -<1-> 1_3_a{Einstein's solid}	
    -<1-> 1_4_a{Boltzmann & Maxwell-Boltzmann distributions}
    -<1-> 1_5_a{Classical Averages}
    -<1-> 1_6_a{Quantum Distributions}
    -<1-> 1_7_a{The Quantum Gas}

    -<1-> 1_8_a{Photon Gas}
	-<1-> 1_9_a{The Laser}
	-<1-> 1_10_a{Specific Heats}
	-<1-> 1_11_a{Debye Model}

--- slide: Specific Heats ---

	- We want to understand how a microscopic object stores energy 
	- Equipartition theorem states that for each degree of freedom for quadratic variables$ ( E \propto x^{2} ) $, the object will have an energy $1 / 2 k_{B} T$:
\[
<E>_{one particle}  = \frac{1}{2} k_{B} T \,, \qquad per~degree~of~freedom
\] 

- The total internal energy $U$ of a piece of material is then
\[
U_{per mole}  = N_{A} \frac{1}{2} k_{B} T = \frac{1}{2} R T \,, \qquad per~degree~of~freedom
\]

- Specific heat may be defined either per mass or per mole

- Molar specific heat capacity would be:
\[
\hspace{8.5cm} C_{V}  = \frac{\partial U_{\text {per mole}}}{\partial T}
\]
- Thus, equipartition theorem links $U$ and $T$ and a provides route to calculate the heat capacity $C_{V}$

--- slide: Specific Heats ---

 (Gases)

{6.5cm}
\small

	- For monoatomic gas molecules, the number of degrees of freedom comes only from translation in 3 directions $(x, y, z)$ resulting in a number of 3 \implies[\small]{ Translational $C_V$: $\quad C_{V}  = 3 \cdot \frac{1}{2} R  = \frac{3}{2} R$ }

- For diatomic gas molecules, rotation becomes significant at certain temperatures (room temperature) contributing two more degrees of freedom 

- At even higher temperature (the shift starts at around $1000 \mathrm{~K}$) also molecular vibrations become relevant, contributing two more degrees of freedom

- In overall, the heat capacity can exhibit values
\[
C_{V}  = \frac{3}{2} R\,, \qquad \frac{5}{2}  R\,, \qquad or \qquad \frac{7}{2} R
\]

[FIGURE: SOM_Figure_28.png]

[FIGURE: Tikz_SOM_Statistical_figures.pdf]

--- slide: Specific Heats ---

 (Set of harmonic oscillators)

	- When determining vibrational specific heat contribution for gas molecules, integration of the Boltzmann distribution does not give a correct answer (condition $E_{n+1} - E_n \ll k_B T$ no longer holds)  
	
	- We need to sum up the states: 
\[
<E>\,  =  \frac{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} n \hbar \omega_{0} e^{-n \hbar \omega_{0} / k_{B} T}}{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} e^{-n \hbar \omega_{0} / k_{B} T}} =\ldots= \frac{\hbar \omega_{0}}{e^{\hbar \omega_{0} / k_{B} T}-1}
\]

- For a mole of harmonic oscillators 
\[
U_{\text {per mole}}^{(vib)}  = N_{A} \frac{\hbar \omega_{0}}{e^{\hbar \omega_{0} / k_{B} T}-1}
\]

- And differentiating:
\[
C_{V}^{(vib)}  = \frac{\partial U_per mole^{(vib)}}{\partial T}
 = N_{A} k_{B} \frac{ ( \hbar \omega_{0} )^{2} e^{\hbar \omega_{0} / k_{B} T} }{ ( k_{B} T ) ^{2} ( e^{\hbar \omega_{0} / k_{B} T}-1 ) ^{2} }
\]

--- slide: Specific Heats ---

	- At high T $ ( k_{B} T \gg \hbar \omega_{0} ) $, the proportionality $<E> \propto k_{B} T$ seems to be valid (degrees of freedom = 2 for vibrations)

[FIGURE: SOM_Figure_29.png]

- At low temperatures $ ( k_{B} T \ll \hbar \omega_{0} )$, mean energy $<E>\,$ rises from zero very slowly. Reason is that few of the molecules would be energetic enough to occupy any state other than the ground state

- The $C_{V}$ shown in Fig.~(b) is practically the slope of $<E>\,$ shown in  Fig.~(a) 

{5cm} \small

- The shift from $C_{V}=0$ to $C_{V}=R$ resembles the shift in Fig.~28, where increased  temperature enables the population of vibrational states 

- This takes place apparently already at around: 
\[
k_{B} T  = 0.3 \;...\; 0.5 \hbar \omega_{0}
\]

--- slide: Specific Heats ---

- For hydrogen molecule, the resonance frequency is $\omega_{0}=8.28 \times 10^{14}~\mathrm{rad} / \mathrm{s}$, giving significant rise at temperature $T=0.3 \frac{\hbar \omega_{0}}{k_{B}} \approx 1900 \mathrm{~K}$ 
- This agrees remarkably well with Fig.~28

[FIGURE: SOM_Figure_28.png]

--- slide: Specific Heats ---

 (Solid state)

	- In solids, atoms/electrons cannot move freely or rotate 
	- They can only oscillate/vibrate in three directions 
	- Since one-dimensional oscillator has 2 degrees of freedom, three-dimensional solid exhibits 6 degrees of freedom resulting in 
\[
C_{V}  =  6 \cdot \frac{1}{2} R  = 3 R \,, \qquad (dielectrics)
\]

- In conducting materials (e.\,g. metals), conduction electrons are not bound to individual atoms, but move around acting like a gas

- Classically thinking, conduction electrons have 3 translational degrees of freedom, contributing by an additional factor of $3 \times \frac{1}{2}=1.5$ to the heat capacity $C_V$
\implies{ classically one expects conductors to have $C_{V}  =  4.5 R$ }

--- slide: Specific Heats ---

 (Solid state)

	- It turns out that the classical expectations given above are not fulfilled 
	
	- For many solids, the value of heat capacity is still close to $3R$ ($C_V \approx 3R$), both for insulators and for conductors

- The electrons in conductors are packed to lowest states, and since at

{6.4cm}

- room temperature, the value of $k_{B} T$ is much less than $E_{F}$, practically, the situation is quite the same as $T=0$ would be

- In conductors only a small fraction of electrons move, causing a negligible contribution to the heat capacity $C_V$

[FIGURE: SOM_Statistical_Table_3.png]

--- slide: Specific Heats ---

 (Solid state)

- The atoms in a solid are strongly coupled (see Figure)

- One cannot displace a single atom without disturbing nearby atoms (and the whole solid)

{7.8cm}

	-  \small
	- Must consider collective vibrational excitations 
	- Resulting acoustic waves behave like a boson gas
	- These waves are called phonons and act as bosonic quanta of atomic vibrations
	- A phonon has a zero spin, it may travel inside the solid carrying an energy of $h f$ ($\sim$10 meV)
	- A phonon is not a 'real particle' in the same sense as a photon is, but it is a 'quasiparticle' or a 'collective excitation' 
	\implies[\small]{ Phonons only exist inside the solid }

## 1.11 Debye Model

--- slide: 1. Statistical Mechanics ---

    -<1-> 1_1_a{A Simple Thermodynamic System}
    -<1-> 1_2_a{Entropy and Temperature}	
    -<1-> 1_3_a{Einstein's solid}	
    -<1-> 1_4_a{Boltzmann & Maxwell-Boltzmann distributions}
    -<1-> 1_5_a{Classical Averages}
    -<1-> 1_6_a{Quantum Distributions}
    -<1-> 1_7_a{The Quantum Gas}

    -<1-> 1_8_a{Photon Gas}
	-<1-> 1_9_a{The Laser}
	-<1-> 1_10_a{Specific Heats}
	-<1-> 1_11_a{Debye Model}

--- slide: Debye Model ---

	- There is a lower limit to phonon wavelengths 
	\implies{In practice: \qquad $\frac{1}{2} \lambda_{\min}  = a=$ the lattice constant } 
	
	- A minimum wavelength indicates maximum frequency and maximum energy $E_{\max }$ for the phonons

{8.5cm}

	- The speed of phonons is not the speed of light, but rather the speed of sound $v_{s}$ in the material

- Phonons can oscillate along two transverse directions, but also longitudinal vibrations are allowed

- With equal speeds for both transverse and longitudinal oscillations, the phononic density of states must be multiplied by $3 / 2$

[FIGURE: SOM_Figure_30_cropped.png]

--- slide: Debye Model ---

	- Now we can write the formula for the energy of a phonon system:
\[
U_{\text {phonon }}  = \int_{0}^{E_{\max}} E \mathbb{N}(E) D(E) d E
 = 
 \int_{0}^{E_{\max }} E \frac{1}{e^{E / k_{B} T}-1} \frac{12 \pi V}{h^{3} v_{S}{ }^{3}} E^{2} d E
\]

- To perform the integral, we need an estimate for the maximum

{7.3cm}

	 - energy $E_{\max }$, and link it to the smallest wavelength $2 a$

- Consider the atoms in a cubic volume of a crystalline solid 

- Number of atoms on one edge is $N^{1 / 3}$, and spacing between atoms is $L / N^{1 / 3}$ 
- \implies{ Solid supports phonons, with the smallest phonon wavelength being $2 L / N^{1 / 3}$}

[FIGURE: SOM_Figure_31.png]

--- slide: Debye Model ---

 \fontsize{11.5}{13}\selectfont 

	- Due to the periodicity of the solid, the allowed wave vector $\mathbf{k}$ values are discretized: a single phononic state occupies a volume of  $( 2 \pi /L )^{3}$ (in the so-called $\mathbf{k}$-space/state space)

{8cm} 
 \fontsize{11.5}{13}\selectfont 

- Note, $\mathbf{k}$ is basically a vector (k=$|\mathbf{k}|$), it may point at any direction, allowed phononic states form a sphere!

- The system has in total $N$ states, which must be equal to the product of the density and the volume of states (in $\mathbf{k}$-space):
\[

N & =  \bigg( \frac{L}{2\pi} \bigg)^{3} \bigg( \frac{4 \pi}{3} k_{\max }^{3} \bigg)  \\
\Rightarrow \qquad k_{\max } & =  \bigg( \frac{8\pi^3}{L^3} \frac{3N}{4\pi} \bigg) ^{1 / 3} \\
& =  \bigg( \frac{6 \pi^{2} N}{L^{3}} \bigg) ^{1 / 3}  =  \bigg( \frac{6 \pi^{2} N}{V} \bigg) ^{1 / 3}

\]

[FIGURE: SOM_Figure_32.png]

--- slide: Debye Model ---

	- Having derived the maximum wave number, we can now estimate maximum energy of the phonons:
\[
E_{\max }  =  \frac{h v_{s} k_{\max }}{2 \pi}  =  \frac{h v_{s}}{2 \pi} ( \frac{6 \pi^{2} N}{V} ) ^{1 / 3}  =  h v_{s} ( \frac{3 N}{4 \pi V} ) ^{1 / 3} \,, \qquad E=\hbar \omega ~and~\omega = v_s k
\]

- Here, we have assumed that the speed of the propagating waves in the solid is the same as the speed of sound, verified experimentally, and that all phonons move with same speed 

- Setting $E_{\max }=k_{B} T_{D}$ we get the maximum energy expressed in units of temperature, with so-called Debye temperature , which obviously depends on properties of the given material, such as $N / V$ and $v_{s}$:
\[
T_{D}  = \frac{E_{\max }}{k_{B}}  = \frac{h v_{S}}{k_{B}} \bigg( \frac{3 N}{4 \pi V} \bigg) ^{1 / 3}
\]

--- slide: Debye Model ---

	- With the maximum energy derived, we can return to the original integral for the total energy of the system:
	$$
	U_{\text {phonon}}  =   \int_{0}^{k_{B} T_{D}} E \frac{1}{e^{E / k_{B} T}-1} \frac{9 N}{ ( k_{B} T_{D} ) ^{3}} E^{2} d E  = 9 k_{B} N \frac{T^{4}}{T_{D}^{3}} \int_{0}^{T_{D} / T} \frac{x^{3}}{e^{x}-1} d x
	$$
	where we have changed the variable $E$ into $x$ $(x \equiv E/k_{B} T)$
- For a mole of material $N=N_{A}$, and since $k_{B} N_{A}=R$, we obtain finally:
\[
U_{phonon,\,per mole}  = 9 R \frac{T^{4}}{T_{D}^{3}} \int_{0}^{T_{D} / T} \frac{x^{3}}{e^{x}-1} d x \qquad
\]

- Unfortunately, the integral cannot be calculated analytically, we need to rely on numerical integration, and compare with experimental data

--- slide: Debye Model ---

	- The Debye model described above, fits well with experimental data on heat capacities, as is seen in the below Figure
	
	- The derivation was quick\;\&\;dirty, rigorous one will be given in our introductory course on solid-state physics!

[FIGURE: SOM_Figure_33.png]

--- slide: Entropia ja Lämpötila ---

 (Entropia)

	- Entropia $S$ on suure joka on verrannollinen  'eri tapojen' $W$ logaritmiin (ts. multiplisiteetin $\Omega$ logaritmiin). Boltzmannin vakio $k_B$ määrittelee entropian $S$ yksiköt
	\[
S  = k_{B} \ln W \qquad \& \qquad S  =  k_{B} \ln \Omega \qquad \& \qquad [S]=\frac{J}{K}
\]

- Aiemmin näimme että systeemi pyrkii kehittymään kohti tasapainotilaa, ts. kohti suurinta $W$:n arvoa

- Termodynamiikan toisen pääsäännön mukaan, (suljetun systeemin) entropia kasvaa: 
\[
\Delta S \geq 0
\]
Tarkastellaan tilojen lukumäärää $W$: 
\[
\Delta S  = S_{f}  - S_{i}  = k_{B} [  \ln W_{f} -\ln W_{i} ]  = k_{B} \ln \frac{W_{f}}{W_{i}} 
\]

--- slide: Entropia ja Lämpötila ---

 (Entropia suljetussa systeemissä)

\footnote{{Kertaa eristetyn, suljetun, ja avoimen systeemin määritelmät.}}
	
{8.5cm}

	- Tarkastellaan kahta kappaletta, 1 ja 2, termisessä kontaktissa. Lämpöä siirtyy ja entropia muuttuu:
\[ 
 d S_{t o t} = d S_{1}  + d S_{2} 
 \] 
- Oletetaan että entropia muuttuu vain energianmuutoksen takia (ok, jos vain lämpö 

- siirtyy, ei esim. hiukkaset), joten $S=S(E)$,~jolloin
\[ 
 d S =  \bigg( \frac{\partial S}{\partial E} \bigg) \,d E 
 \] 
- Eli $d S_{t o t} = ( \frac{\partial S}{\partial E} )_{2} d E_{2}  +  (  \frac{\partial S}{\partial E} )_{1} d E_{1}$,
ja koska energiaa siirtyy vain kappaleiden 1 ja 2 välillä, ehto $d E_{1}=-d E_{2}$ johtaa yhtälöön
\[ 
 d S_{tot} =   [   \bigg( \frac{\partial S}{\partial E}  \bigg)_{2} -  \bigg( \frac{\partial S}{\partial E}  \bigg)_{1}  \;] d E_{2}
 \]

[FIGURE: SOM_Figure_4.png]

--- slide: Entropia ja Lämpötila ---

 (Lämpötilan ja entropian yhteys)

	- Tasapainossa kappaleilla on sama lämpötila

- Tiedämme myös että tasapainotilassa entropia $S$ ei enää kasva, eli $d S_{t o t}=0$ mistä seuraa 
\[ 
  \bigg(  \frac{\partial S}{\partial E}  \bigg)_{2} =   \bigg( \frac{\partial S}{\partial E}  \bigg)_{1} 
 \]
- Tämä ehto johtaa näppärään lämpötilan määritelmään: 

- 
	- Tasapainossa lämpötilat $T_1  =   T_2 \equiv T$ 
	-  Lämpötila $T$ voidaan siis määritellä yhtälöllä 
	 \[
	 \frac{1}{T} \equiv \bigg( \frac{\partial S}{\partial E} \bigg)\,, \qquad \,~missä \qquad S  = k_{B} \ln W
	 \]

--- slide: Entropia ja Lämpötila ---

- Mitä tarkoittaa entropian $S$ derivaatta energian $E$ suhteen? 

	- Fysikaalisessa systeemissä tilojen lukumäärä ($W$) riippuu energiasta (suurempi energia, suurempi multiplisiteetti). Fysikaalisesti järkevää derivoida

- Usein $W=W(E)$ eli myös entropia $S=S(E)$
- Koska energian muutos $dE$ on kappaleiden välillä siirtyneen lämmön määrä ($dE=dQ$),  saadaan
\[
	 \frac{1}{T}  =   \frac{\partial S}{\partial E}  \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad dS  =  \frac{dQ}{T} 
	 \]
minkä avulla entropian muutokselle $\Delta S$ saadaan relaatio
\[ 
 \Delta S  = S_{2}  -  S_{1} = \int_{i}^{f} \frac{d Q}{T} 
 \]

--- slide: Klassiset keskiarvot ---

 (Molekyylien vauhtijakauma)

	- Tarkastellaan seuraavaksi ideaalikaasusta koostuvaa systeemiä, jossa yksittäisen molekyylin energia on
\[
E  = \frac{1}{2} m ( v_{x}^{2}+v_{y}^{2}+v_{z}^{2} )  = \frac{1}{2} m v^{2}
\]

- Oletetaan että molekyylien nopeuksille on olemassa jakauma, mutta emme vielä tiedä jakauman tarkkaa muotoa

- Löytääksemme mielivaltaisen keskinopeutta kuvaavan funktion $f=f(v)$, meidän täytyy integroida 3D-tilavuudessa olevien molekyylien nopeuksien yli, eli nopeusvektorilla on 3 komponenttia

--- slide: Klassiset keskiarvot ---

 (Molekyylien vauhtijakauma)

- Integrointi tilavuuden $V$ yli sieventyy pois, jolloin saadaan:
\[
\overline{f(v)}  = \frac{ \int f(v) e^{-\frac{1}{2} m v^{2} / k_{B} T} d V' d v_{x} d v_{y} d v_{z}}{ \int e^{-\frac{1}{2} m v^{2} / k_{B} T} d V' d v_{x} d v_{y} d v_{z} }  = \frac{ \int f(v) e^{-\frac{1}{2} m v^{2} / k_{B} T} d v_{x} d v_{y} d v_{z}}{ \int e^{-\frac{1}{2} m v^{2} / k_{B} T} d v_{x} d v_{y} d v_{z}}
\]
- Koska haemme siis vauhtijakaumaa (ei nopeus), on hyödyllistä siirtyä karteesisesta kordinaatistosta pallo koordinaatistoon käyttäen muunnosta: 
\[
d v_{x} d v_{y} d v_{z}  = v^{2} d v \sin \theta \,d \theta \,d \phi
\]

--- slide: Klassiset keskiarvot ---

 (Todennäköisyysjakauma)

	- Näin integraali muuttuu muotoon
	\[
	\overline{f(v)}  =  \frac{ \int f(v) e^{-\frac{1}{2} m v^{2} / k_{B} T} v^{2} d v \sin \theta d \theta d  \phi }{ \int e^{-\frac{1}{2} m v^{2} / k_{B} T} v^{2} d v \sin \theta d \theta d  \phi }
	 =  \frac{ \int_{0}^{\infty} f(v) e^{-\frac{1}{2} m v^{2} / k_{B} T} v^{2} d v}{ \int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-\frac{1}{2} m v^{2} / k_{B} T} v^{2} d v}
	\]
	
 missä nimittäjän integraalit, ja muuttujien $\theta$ ja $\phi$  yli tapahtuvat integraalit voidaan ratkaista johtaen relaatioon
\[
\overline{f(v)}  =  \int_{0}^{\infty}  f(v) [ \sqrt{2/\pi} \bigg( \frac{m}{k_{B} T}  \bigg)^{3 / 2} v^{2} e^{-\frac{1}{2} m v^{2} / k_{B} T}] d v
\]

- Kaavan integrandin nähdään nyt muistuttavan todennäköisyysjakaumaa $P=P(v)$:
\[
\overline{f(v)}  =  \int f(v) d P =  \int f(v) \frac{d P}{d v} d v
\]

--- slide: Klassiset keskiarvot ---

 (Maxwellin–Boltzmannin vauhtijakauma)

- Näin sulkujen sisällä oleva termi voidaan tulkita kaasumolekyylien vauhtien todennäköisyysjakaumaksi
\[
P(v)_{\text {Maxwell}}  =  \frac{d P}{d v}  =  \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}   \bigg( \frac{m}{k_{B} T} \bigg)^{3 / 2} v^{2} e^{-\frac{1}{2} m v^{2} / k_{B} T}
\]

- Jos me lisäämme molekyylien tilatiheyden $n=N / V$ kaavaan, jakauman yksiköksi saadaan hiukkasta/yksikkötilavuus, mutta jakauma sisältää informaatiota molekyylien vauhtien todennäköisyysjakaumasta
\[
n(v)_{\text {Maxwell }}  = \frac{N}{V} \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}   \bigg( \frac{m}{k_{B} T} \bigg)^{3 / 2} v^{2} e^{-\frac{1}{2} m v^{2} / k_{B} T}
\]
Tätä jakaumaa kutsutaan Maxwellin vauhtijakaumaksi, tai\\
Maxwellin–Boltzmannin vauhtijakaumaksi

--- slide: Klassiset keskiarvot ---

 (Maxwellin–Boltzmannin vauhtijakauma)

[FIGURE: SOM_Figure_8.png]

{7.5cm}
	
- Huomaa että vauhtijakauma kasvaa ylöspäin nollavauhdista (kuva~8)

- Alkuun todennäköisyys kasvaa tilan energian kasvaessa, koska jakauman neliöllinen nopeusriippuvuus hallitsee käytöstä

- Korkeammilla tiloilla/nopeuksilla mie\-hi\-tyksen toden\-näköi\-syys alkaa selkeästi vaimenemaan eksponentiaalisesti energian funktiona

-<1->
$$
n(v)_{\text {Maxwell }}  = \frac{N}{V} \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}  \bigg( \frac{m}{k_{B} T} \bigg)^{3 / 2} v^{2} e^{-\frac{1}{2} m v^{2} / k_{B} T}
$$

 (Bosonit vs. fermionit)

	- Kvanttimekaniikasta muistamme että aaltofunktioiden täytyy toteuttaa seuraavat ehdot:

- Bosonit: {\small Identtisten bosonien aaltofunktioiden täytyy olla symmetrisiä kahden bosonin vaihdon suhteen. Oletetaan kaksi identtistä bosonia yksihiukkastiloissa $n$ ja $n'$ ja kuvataan näitä tiloja aaltofunktioilla $\psi_{n}$ ja $\psi_{n'}$. Jos merkitsemme bosonit 1 ja 2, yhdistetyn kahden hiukkasen systeemin tilaa kuvataan aaltofunktiolla joka on muotoa $\psi_{n}(1) \psi_{n'}(2)$.
 Mutta koska hiukkaset ovat erottamattomia, voisimme kirjoittaa systeemin aaltofunktion muotoon $\psi_{n}(2) \psi_{n'}(1)$. Emme voi tietää onko bosoni 1 (vai 2) tilalla $n$ vai $n'$. Koko systeemin aaltofunktion täytyy olla symmetrinen:}
\[
\psi_{B E}(1,2)  = \psi_{S}  = \psi_{n}(1) \psi_{n'}(2)  + \psi_{n'}(1) \psi_{n}(2) \, \qquad (symmetrinen)
\]

- Fermionit: {\small Identtisten fermionien aaltofunktion pitää olla antisymmetrinen:} 
\[
\quad \psi_{F D}(1,2)  = \psi_{A}  = \psi_{n}(1) \psi_{n^{\prime}}(2)  - \psi_{n'}(1) \psi_{n}(2) \, \qquad (antisymmetrinen)
\]

&\Rightarrow& \qquad E_{F}^{3 / 2}  &=   \bigg( \frac{\pi^{2} \hbar^{2}}{m}  \bigg)^{3 / 2} \frac{3}{(2 s+1) \pi 2^{1/2}} \frac{N}{V}  =  \frac{3 \pi^{2}}{2^{1/2}} \frac{\hbar^{3}}{(2 s+1) m^{3 / 2}} \frac{N}{V}

\]
- Eli on olemassa verrannollisuus $\frac{\hbar^{3}}{(2 s+1) m^{3 / 2}} \frac{N}{V} \propto E_{F}^{3 / 2}$ mitä voidaan hyödyntää analysoimaan kvanttitermin merkitystä keskimääräisessä energiassa:
\[
\overline{E}  = \frac{3}{2} k_{B} T [\! 1 \mp \frac{\pi^{3} \hbar^{3} \sqrt{2}}{(2 s+1) (2 \pi m k_{B} T )^{\frac{3}{2}} (\frac{N}{V} ) }  +\ldots ]
\]

&tai kun\quad& & \\

\]

	-<1-> 1_10_a{Ominaislämpökapasiteetti}
	-<1-> 1_11_a{Debyen Malli}

--- slide: Fotonikaasu ---

 (Tilatiheys)

	- Toisin kuin elektronit (ja He-4 atomit), fotonit ovat bosoneita (spin $=1$) 
	
	- Fotonikaasun kuvaamiseksi meidän täytyy laskea fotonien tilatiheys

- Fotonien energia liittyy suoraan niiden taajuuteen $f$ (eikä niinkään energiatason kvanttilukuun $n$): $ \qquad E =h f  = h \frac{c}{\lambda} $

{8cm}

	- Tarkastellaan aluksi 3D-kvanttikaivoa jonka leveys on  $L$, mihin mahtuu pie\-nim\-millään $\lambda=2 L$ kokoinen kvantti/fotoni

[FIGURE: SOM_Tipler_figure.png]

	\[

n_{x}^{2} + n_{y}^{2} + n_{z}^{2}  =  n^{2}  \qquad \myeq \qquad  R^{2}  =   \bigg( \frac{L}{\lambda / 2}  \bigg)^{2}

\]
- jonka avulla tilojen $n$ täyttämän pallon säde tila-avaruudessa  voidaan laskea:
\[
R  =  \frac{L}{\lambda / 2}  =  \frac{2 L}{\lambda}  =  \frac{2 L E}{h c}
\]

--- slide: Fotonikaasu ---

 (Tilatiheys)

	- Montako 'hilapistettä' mahtuu $n$-avaruuden palloon jonka säde on  $R$?
\[

& & R& = \frac{2 L E}{h c} \\ 
&\Rightarrow \qquad& N &= 2 \cdot \frac{1}{8} \cdot \frac{4}{3} \pi R^{3}  =  \frac{\pi}{3}  \bigg( \frac{2 L E}{h c}  \bigg)^{3}  
=  \frac{8 \pi}{3} \frac{E^{3}}{h^{3} c^{3}} L^{3}  
= \frac{8 \pi}{3} \frac{E^{3}}{h^{3} c^{3}} V

\]

- missä $N$ on tilojen lukumäärä säteeltään $R=2 L E /(h c)$ olevan pallon sisällä
- Nyt energiavälillä $d E$ olevien pisteiden lukumäärä voidaan laskea:
\[
\frac{d N}{d E}  = \frac{8 \pi}{3} \frac{3 E^{2}}{h^{3} c^{3}} V
 = \frac{8 \pi E^{2}}{h^{3} c^{3}} V 
 \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad 
 \frac{d N}{d f}  = \frac{8 \pi f^{2}}{c^{3}} V \qquad (E=h f \quad \Rightarrow \quad d E=h d f)
\]

--- slide: Fotonikaasu ---

 (Tilatiheys)

	- Eli olemme laskeneet tilojen lukumäärän jotka ovat tietyllä taajuusvälillä:
\[
\frac{\text { differentiaalinen tilojen lukumäärä taajuusvälillä }}{d f}  = \frac{8 \pi V}{c^{3}} f^{2}
\]
- Tai vastaava energian avulla ilmaistuna:
\[
\frac{\text { differentiaalinen tilojen lukumäärä energiavälillä dE }}{d E / h}  = \frac{8 \pi V}{c^{3}} (E / h)^{2}
\]

- Eli fotonikaasun tilatiheydeksi $D=D(E)$ (tilavuudessa $V$) saadaan:
\[
D(E)  = \frac{8 \pi V}{h^{3} c^{3}} E^{2}
\]

--- slide: Fotonikaasu ---

 (Miehitysluvun normeeraus)

	- Fotonikaasu eroaa harmonisen värähtelijän tapauksesta, tai esim. nestemäisen heliumin tapauksesta

- Fotonikaasussa fotonien lukumäärää ei ole etukäteen rajoitettu, systeemi absoboi ja emittoi fotoneita koko ajan, fotonien hetkellinen lukumäärä määräytyy systeemin tilan ja sen ominaisuuksien mukaan

- Bosen–Einsteinin statistiikka bosoneille oli muotoa: 
\[ \mathbb{N} (E)_{B E}  =  \frac{1}{B e^{E / k_{B} T}-1}
\] 
- missä parametri $B$ täytyisi liittää hiukkasten lukumäärään $N$ 

- Koska fotonikaasussa $N$ on vapaa muuttuja, ratkaisu on määritellä $B\equiv1$ (fotonikaasulle)
\[
 \mathbb{N} (E)_{B E, \text {fotonikaasu}}=\frac{1}{e^{E / k_{B} T}-1}
\]

--- slide: Fotonikaasu ---

 (Esimerkki)

	- K: Vertaa kvantitatiivisesti sähkömagneettisen säteilyn ja aineen sisäisiä energiatiloja
- A: Voisimme laskea keskimääräiset energiat: 
\[
<E>\,  =  \frac{\int E  \mathbb{N} (E) D(E) d E}{\int  \mathbb{N} (E) D(E) d E}
\]
- missä osoittaja vastaa systeemin energiaa (ja nimittäjä vastaa hiukkasten lukumäärää) 

- Voisimme myös vain käyttää osoittajaa SMG-säteilyn (eli fotonikaasun) sisäisen energian laskemiseksi 

--- slide: Fotonikaasu ---

 (Esimerkki)

- Käyttämällä muuttujanvaihdosta $E  = k_{B} T x \quad \Rightarrow \quad x  = E/(k_{B} T)$, osoittajaksi saadaan
\[

U_{fotoni}  &=  \int_{0}^{\infty} E  \mathbb{N} (E) D(E) d E
 =  
 \int_{0}^{\infty} E \frac{1}{e^{E / k_{B} T}-1} \frac{8 \pi V}{h^{3} c^{3}} E^{2} d E \\
& = 
\frac{8 \pi V}{h^{3} c^{3}} \int_{0}^{\infty} k_{B} T x \frac{1}{e^{x}-1} ( k_{B} T x ) ^{2} k_{B} T d x
 = 
 \frac{8 \pi V k_{B}^{4} T^{4}}{h^{3} c^{3}} \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{x^{3} d x}{e^{x}-1}  \\
 &= 
 \frac{8 \pi V k_{B}^{4} T^{4}}{h^{3} c^{3}} \cdot \frac{\pi^{4}}{15}

\]

--- slide: Fotonikaasu ---

 (Esimerkki)

	- Fotonikaasulle saadaan siis
	\[
	U_{fotoni}=\frac{8 \pi V k_{B}^{4} T^{4}}{h^{3} c^{3}} \cdot \frac{\pi^{4}}{15}  = \frac{8 \pi^{5} V k_{B}^{4} T^{4}}{15 h^{3} c^{3}}
	\]
- $300 \mathrm{~K}$ lämpötilassa arvoksi saadaan: $\quad \frac{U_{fotoni}}{V} =6,1 \times 10^{-6} \mathrm{~J} / \mathrm{m}^{3}$

- Tavallisille kaasuille (yksiatominen ideaalikaasu) sisäenergiaksi NTP:ssä saadaan
\[
\frac{U_{\text {aine}}}{V}
 = 
 \frac{N \times \frac{3}{2} k_{B} T}{V}
  = 
  \frac{\frac{3}{2} p V}{V} = \frac{3}{2} p = 1,5 \times 10^{5} \mathrm{~J} / \mathrm{m}^{3}
\]
- Eli suhde on pieni: $\frac{U_{\text {fotoni}}}{U_{\text {aine}}}=4 \times 10^{-11}$

 \implies{ Eli NTP-olosuhteissa suhde on sellainen että
 		systeemin termo-\\
		dynaamisesta kokonaisenergiasta erittäin pieni osa on \\
		 SMG-säteilyä }

--- slide: Fotonikaasu ---

 (Planckin laki, musta kappale ja Wienin siirtymälaki)

	- Edellisen esimerkin sisäenergiaa kuvaavan integraalin integrandi voidaan ajatella systeemin kokonaisenergiana tietyllä fotonienergian välillä [[$E , E+d E$]]:
\[
d U_{fotoni}(E, E+d E)
 = 
 E \frac{1}{e^{E / k_{B} T}-1} \frac{8 \pi V}{h^{3} c^{3}} E^{2} d E
  = \frac{h f^{3}}{e^{h f / k_{B} T}-1} \frac{8 \pi V}{c^{3}} d f
\]
- tämä on Planckin laki,  joka antaa meille

 (Sironta- ja absorptioprosessit)

{7.2cm}
\small 

	- Kuinka kuvata prosesseja matemaattisesti?
Yksinkertaisuuden vuoksi tarkastellaan kaasumaista vahvistinainetta (energiatilatarkastelu yksinkertaisempaa kuin kiinteässä aineessa) 

- Spontaanissa emissiossa transitionopeuden $R_{spon}$  täytyy olla verrannollinen virittyneiden ato\-mi\-en lukumäärään, $N_{2}$, jolloin verrannollisuuskertoi\-meksi saadaan $A_{spon}$ ($R_{spon} = A_{\text {spon}} N_2$)

- Absorptionopeus on verrannollinen alemmalla tasolla olevien hiukkasten lukumäärään $N_{1}$, sekä oikean energian omaavien fotonien lukumäärään $Y(\Delta E)$, missä $\Delta E=E_{2}-E_{1}$. Verrannollisuusvakio on $B_{abs}$ ($R_{abs} = B_{abs} N_{1} Y(\Delta E)$)

- Stimuloitu emissio on verrannollinen ylemmän tilan omaavien vapaiden atomien lukumäärään, sekä oikean energian omaavien fotonien lukumäärään, jotka voivat ajaa emissioprossia ($R_{stim}=B_{stim} N_{2} Y(\Delta E)$)

[FIGURE: SOM_Figure_22.png]

--- slide: Laseri ---

 (Einsteinin kertoimet)

	- Kertoimet $A_{\text {spon}}$, $B_{abs}$ ja $B_{\text {stim}}$ tunnetaan nimellä Einsteinin kertoimet 
	
	- Tasapainossa näillä prosesseille pätee:
\[

& & R_{\text {spon}} +R_{\text {stim}}  &= R_{abs} \\
 &\Rightarrow& \qquad A_{\text {spon}} N_{2} +B_{\text {stim}} N_{2} Y(\Delta E) & = B_{abs} N_{1} Y(\Delta E) \qquad \quad 

\]

- Mikä voidaan ratkaista $Y(\Delta E)$ suhteen, ja käyttämällä $\frac{N_{1}}{N_{2}}  = \frac{e^{-\frac{E_{1}}{k_{B} T}}}{e^{-\frac{E_{2}}{k_{B} T}}}$, saadaan:
\[

&\Rightarrow& 
\qquad Y(\Delta E) & = \frac{A_{\text {spon}} / B_{abs}}{\frac{N_{1}}{N_{2}}  - \frac{B_{\text {stim}}}{B_{abs}}}
 = 
 \frac{A_{\text {spon}} / B_{abs}}{\frac{e^{-E_{1} / k_{B} T}}{e^{-E_{2} / k_{B} T}}  - \frac{B_{stim}}{B_{abs}}} \\
 
&\Rightarrow&
 Y(\Delta E) & = \frac{A_{\text {spon}} / B_{abs}}{e^{\Delta E / k_{B} T}  - \frac{B_{\text {stim}}}{B_{abs}}}
  = 
  \frac{A_{\text {spon}} / B_{\text {stim}}}{\frac{B_{abs}}{B_{stim}} e^{\Delta E / k_{B} T}-1}  \qquad \quad 

\]

--- slide: Laseri ---

 (Einsteinin kertoimet)

	- Parametri $Y(\Delta E)$ ei tarkalleen ottaen ole atomeiden kanssa vuorovaikuttavien fotonien lukumäärä, vaan fotonikaasun spektrinen energiatiheys (oikealla energiavälillä), joka noudattaa Planckin lakia 
	
	- Eli voidaan muodostaa kaksi yhtälöä:
\[

Y(\Delta E) &= \frac{A_{\text {spon}} / B_{abs}}{\frac{N_{1}}{N_{2}} - \frac{B_{\text {stim}}}{B_{abs}}}
 = 
 \frac{A_{spon} / B_{abs}}{\frac{e^{-E_{1} / k_{B} T}}{e^{-E_{2} / k_{B} T}}  - \frac{B_{\text {stim}}}{B_{abs}}}
  = 
  \frac{A_{\text {spon}} / B_{abs}}{e^{\Delta E / k_{B} T} - \frac{B_{\text {stim}}}{B_{\text {abs }}}}
   = 
   \frac{A_{\text {spon}} / B_{\text {stim}}}{\frac{B_{abs}}{B_{\text {stim}}} e^{\Delta E / k_{B} T}-1} \\
d U_{fotoni}(\Delta E) &= 
 \frac{h f^{3}}{e^{\frac{\Delta E}{k_{B} T}}-1} \frac{8 \pi V}{c^{3}} d f \quad \Rightarrow \quad \frac{1}{V} \frac{d U_{fotoni}}{d f}
  = \frac{8 \pi}{c^{3}} \frac{h f^{3}}{e^{\frac{\Delta E}{k_{B} T}}-1}  =  Y(\Delta E)

\]
- Jolloin tasapainossa saadaan muodostettua ehto:
\[
B_{\text {stim}} = B_{abs} \qquad jolloin \qquad \frac{A_{\text {spon}}}{B_{\text {stim}}}  = \frac{8 \pi h f^{3}}{c^{3}}
\]

--- slide: Laseri ---

 (Populaatioinversio)

	- Ehto $B_{stim}=B_{abs}$ tarkoittaa että on yhtä todennäköistä että tuleva fotoni absorboituu tai stimuloi emission

- Tämä ehto ei ole laseroinnin kannalta hyödyllinen, stimuloidun emission pitäisi dominoida, koska haluamme valon vahvistumista, jonka stimuloitu emissio mahdollistaa

[FIGURE: Tikz_SOM_Statistical_figures.pdf]

--- slide: Laseri ---

 (Populaatioinversio)

	- Normaalissa lämpötilassa, tasapainossa, lähes kaikki atomit ovat perustilassa 
	- Eli $N_{1} \gg N_{2}$, jolloin prosessien nopeuksille pätee $N_{1} B_{abs} \gg N_{2} B_{stim}$  
	\implies{ Stimuloitu emissio ei dominoi }

- Pumppaamalla (optisesti) perustilassa olevia atomeita voidaan virittää korkeammille energiatiloille 
\implies{ Korkeamman energiatason $E_{3}$ populaatio kasvaa }

- Jos vahvistinaineessa (usein kiinteä, läpinäkyvä kide) on metastabiili tila  $E_{2}$, niin useat tilalla $E_{3}$ olevat atomit relaksoituvat tälle metastabiilille tilalle $E_{2}$ 
\implies{ On mahdollista saavuttaa tilanne $N_{1}<N_{2}$ }

--- slide: Laseri ---

 (Populaatioinversio)

	- Ensin saavutetaan pumppaamalla tilanne jossa $N_{1} \approx N_{2} \approx N_{3}$ (keskimmäinen kuva)
	- Kun pumppaus lopetetaan, tilan $E_{3}$ atomit alkavat relaksoitumaan kasvattaen tilan $E_{2}$ populaatiota, johtaen tilanteeseen $N_{1}<N_{2}$ (kuva oikealla)
	
	- Tätä ilmiötä/ehtoa kutsutaan populaatioinversioksi 

{3.8cm}

	 - Kun fotonikaasu alkaa stimuloimaan metastabiilin tilan $E_{2}$ atomeita, sti\-mu\-loidun emission nopeus alkaa kasvamaan

 (Energian tasanjakautumisen periaate)

	- Selvitetään kuinka systeemit varastoivat energiaa mikrotasolla 
	- Energian tasanjakautumisen periaate kertoo että jokaisen neliöllisesti E:sta riippuvan muuttujan jokainen vapausaste $( E \propto x^{2} ) $, varastoi $\frac{1}{2} k_{B} T$ verran energiaa:
\[
<E>_{yksi hiukkanen}  = \frac{1}{2} k_{B} T \,, \qquad per~vapausaste
\] 

- Koko materiaalin sisäinen energia $U$ on tällöin

\[
U_{per mooli}  = N_{A} \frac{1}{2} k_{B} T = \frac{1}{2} R T \,, \qquad per~vapausaste
\]

- Lämpökapasiteetti määritellään joko massan tai moolin suhteen

- Moolinen ominaislämpökapasiteetti on muotoa 
\[
\hspace{10.0cm} C_{V}  = \frac{\partial U_{\text {per mooli}}}{\partial T}
\]

- Tasanjakautumisen periaate siis liittää $U$:n ja $T$:n ja mahdollistaa ominaislämpökapasiteetin $C_{V}$ laskemisen

--- slide: Ominaislämpökapasiteetti ---

 (Kaasut)

{6.5cm}

-  Kaksiatomisen kaasun molekyyleillä, rotaatiotilat alkavat vaikuttamaan kor\-keammissa lämpötiloissa (huoneenlämmössä) kasvattaen systeemin vapausasteita kahdella 

- Vielä korkeammissa lämpötiloissa (noin yli $1000 \mathrm{~K}$) myös molekyylien vibraatiot muodostuvat tärkeiksi, kasvattaen vapausasteiden lukumäärää edelleen kahdella

- Ominaislämpökapasiteetti voi siis saada arvoja 
\[
C_{V}  = \frac{3}{2} R\,, \qquad \frac{5}{2}  R\,, \qquad tai \qquad \frac{7}{2} R
\]

 (Harmonisten värähtelijöiden joukko)

	- Kun vibraatiotilojen vaikutusta kaasun ominaislämpökapasiteet-\\ tiin lasketaan, Boltzmannin jakauman integrointi antaa väärän vastauksen (ehto $E_{n+1} - E_n \ll k_B T$ ei päde) 
	
	- Meidän pitää summata tilojen yli: 
\[
<E>\,  =  \frac{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} n \hbar \omega_{0} e^{-n \hbar \omega_{0} / k_{B} T}}{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} e^{-n \hbar \omega_{0} / k_{B} T}} =\ldots= \frac{\hbar \omega_{0}}{e^{\hbar \omega_{0} / k_{B} T}-1}
\]

- Yhdelle moolille harmonisia värähtelijöitä
\[
U_{\text {per mooli}}^{(vib)}  = N_{A} \frac{\hbar \omega_{0}}{e^{\hbar \omega_{0} / k_{B} T}-1}
\]

- joka derivoimalla saadaan (ominais)lämpökapasiteetti
\[
C_{V}^{(vib)}  \equiv \frac{\partial U_per mooli^{(vib)}}{\partial T}
 = N_{A} k_{B} \frac{ ( \hbar \omega_{0} )^{2} e^{\hbar \omega_{0} / k_{B} T} }{ ( k_{B} T ) ^{2} ( e^{\hbar \omega_{0} / k_{B} T}-1 ) ^{2} }
\]

- Kuvassa b hahmoteltu $C_{V}$ vastaa käytännössä $<E>\,$:n derivaattaa (kuva~a)

 (Fononit)

	- Fononien aallonpituuksille on olemassa alaraja
	\implies{Käytännössä: \qquad $\frac{1}{2} \lambda_{\min}  = a=$ hilavakio } 

[FIGURE: SOM_Figure_30_cropped.png]

	- Pienin aallonpituus vastaa suurinta taajuutta
	         eli suurinta energiaa $E_{\max}$

{8.5cm}

	- Fononien nopeus ei ole valon nopeus, vaan äänen nopeus $v_{s}$ aineessa

- Fononit voivat värähdellä kahdessa poikittaisessa suunnassa, sekä pitkittäis-\\ suunnassa

- Olettamalla sekä poikittais- että pitkit\-täis\-fononien nopeudet samoiksi, fononinen tilatiheys täytyy kertoa tekijällä $3 / 2$

--- slide: Debyen Malli ---

 (Fononeihin varastoitunut energia)

	- Fononien varastoimalle kokonaisenergialle voidaan muodostaa yhtälö:
\[
U_{\text {fononi}}  = \int_{0}^{E_{\max}} E \mathbb{N}(E) D(E) d E
 = 
 \int_{0}^{E_{\max }} E \frac{1}{e^{E / k_{B} T}-1} \frac{12 \pi V}{h^{3} v_{S}{ }^{3}} E^{2} d E
\]

- Integraalin laskemiseksi meidän täytyy

{7.3cm}

	 - arvioida suurin energia $E_{max}$ ja yhdistää se pienimpään aallonpituuteen $2 a$

- Tarkastellaan atomeita kuutio\-maisessa kiinteässä kappaleessa 

- Atomeita on yhdellä särmällä $N^{1 / 3}$, ja atomien välinen etäisyys on $L / N^{1 / 3}$ 
- \implies{ Aineessa on fononeita, joiden pienin aallonpituus on $2 L / N^{1 / 3}$}

[FIGURE: SOM_Figure_31.png]

--- slide: Debyen Malli ---

 (Fononien tilatiheys ja energia)

 \fontsize{11.5}{13}\selectfont 

	- Jaksollisuuden takia kiinteässä aineessa sallitut aaltovektorit $\mathbf{k}$ ovat diskreettejä: yksittäinen fononitila vie (ns.~$\mathbf{k}$-avaruudessa/tila-avaruudessa) tilavuuden  $( 2 \pi /L )^{3}$ 

{8cm} 
 \fontsize{11.5}{13}\selectfont 

- Huomaa että $\mathbf{k}$ on vektori (k=$|\mathbf{k}|$), ja voi\\ o\-soit\-taa mielivaltaiseen suuntaan, jolloin sallitut fononitilat muodostavat pallon!

- Systeemissä on yhteensä $N$ tilaa, jonka täytyy vastata tiheyden ja tilavuuden tuloa ($\mathbf{k}$-avaruudessa):
\[

N & =  \bigg( \frac{L}{2\pi} \bigg)^{3} \bigg( \frac{4 \pi}{3} k_{\max }^{3} \bigg)  \\
\Rightarrow \qquad k_{\max } & =  \bigg( \frac{8\pi^3}{L^3} \frac{3N}{4\pi} \bigg) ^{1 / 3} \\
& =  \bigg( \frac{6 \pi^{2} N}{L^{3}} \bigg) ^{1 / 3}  =  \bigg( \frac{6 \pi^{2} N}{V} \bigg) ^{1 / 3}

\]

[FIGURE: SOM_Figure_32.png]

--- slide: Debyen Malli ---

 (Fononien energia ja Debyen lämpötila)

	- Nyt kun suurin aaltoluku on johdettu, voidaan suurin fononin energia arvioida:
\[
E_{\max}  =  \frac{h v_{s} k_{\max }}{2 \pi}  =  \frac{h v_{s}}{2 \pi} \bigg( \frac{6 \pi^{2} N}{V} \bigg) ^{1 / 3}  =  h v_{s} \bigg( \frac{3 N}{4 \pi V} \bigg) ^{1 / 3} \,, \qquad E=\hbar \omega ~ja~\omega = v_s k
\]

- Oletimme että etenevien aaltojen nopeus on sama kuin äänen nopeus aineessa, mikä on kokeellisesti vahvistettu tosiasia. Lisäksi oletimme että kaikki fononit liikkuvat samalla nopeudella 

- Asettamalla $E_{\max }=k_{B} T_{D}$ saamme ilmaistua suurimman energian lämpötilana, niin sanottuna  Debyen lämpötilana, mikä selvästikin riippuu aineen ominaisuuksista, kuten $N / V$ ja $v_{s}$:
\[
T_{D}  = \frac{E_{\max }}{k_{B}}  = \frac{h v_{S}}{k_{B}} \bigg( \frac{3 N}{4 \pi V} \bigg) ^{1 / 3}
\]

--- slide: Debyen Malli ---

 (Fononeihin varastoitunut energia ja Debyen funktio)

	- Nyt kun maksimienergia on laskettu, voimme palata alkuperäiseen kokonaisenergian integraaliin:
	$$
	U_{\text {fononi}}  =   \int_{0}^{k_{B} T_{D}} E \frac{1}{e^{E / k_{B} T}-1} \frac{9 N}{ \big( k_{B} T_{D} \big) ^{3}} E^{2} d E  = 9 k_{B} N \frac{T^{4}}{T_{D}^{3}} \int_{0}^{T_{D} / T} \frac{x^{3}}{e^{x}-1} d x
	$$
	missä muuttuja $E$ on vaihdettu $x$:ksi $(x \equiv E/k_{B} T)$
- Moolille ainetta $N=N_{A}$, ja koska $k_{B} N_{A}=R$, saamme lopuksi:
\[
U_{fononi,\,per mooli}  = 9 R \frac{T^{4}}{T_{D}^{3}} \int_{0}^{T_{D} / T} \frac{x^{3}}{e^{x}-1} d x \qquad
\]

- Valitettavasti Debyen funktion integraalia ei voida analyyttisesti laskea, vaan se täytyy arvioida numeerisesti, ja verrata kokeellisiin tuloksiin
